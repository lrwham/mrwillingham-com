## Warmup: Set Up Your Score

Open MuseScore and build a new score. This takes three minutes and every setting matters.

1. **Create New Score.**
2. Title: **Ode to Joy**. Composer: **Beethoven**.
3. Instrument: **Piano**, or any single treble instrument.
4. Key signature: **C major** — no sharps, no flats.
5. Time signature: **4/4**.
6. Measures: **8**.
7. Save it immediately as **LastName_OdeToJoy**.

Eight measures of 4/4. Thirty-two beats total. That is the container you are about to fill.

## Work Session: Part 1 — Read It First

You are looking at the melody from the last movement of Beethoven's Ninth Symphony. It is about two hundred years old and you have definitely heard it.

**Read it before you touch the computer.** Point at each note and say its name out loud. Then say the rhythm. Find these things before you start typing:

- Where does the melody **step** to the next note, and where does it **skip** over one?
- Which measures are **identical** to earlier measures?
- Where is the only note that is **not** a quarter note?

Measures 1 through 4 and measures 5 through 8 start out exactly the same. Only the ending changes. Noticing that cuts your work almost in half — and it is also the reason the tune sounds finished at the end, which we come back to on Tuesday.

### The Melody

Use this if you get lost reading the notation. Every note is a **quarter note** unless the table says otherwise.

| Measure | Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | E | E | F | G |
| **2** | G | F | E | D |
| **3** | C | C | D | E |
| **4** | E *(dotted quarter)* | D *(eighth)* | D *(half)* | — |
| **5** | E | E | F | G |
| **6** | G | F | E | D |
| **7** | C | C | D | E |
| **8** | D *(dotted quarter)* | C *(eighth)* | C *(half)* | — |

Measures 4 and 8 are the only tricky ones. The dotted quarter takes beat 1 and half of beat 2. The eighth note finishes beat 2. The half note takes beats 3 and 4. Together that is four beats, exactly.

## Work Session: Part 2 — Enter It

1. Press **N** for note input.
2. Choose your duration **before** each note — quarter, eighth, half, or dotted.
3. Type the letter key for the note name, or click it onto the staff.
4. Enter one measure, then **stop and press spacebar**. Listen. Fix it now.

Do not enter all eight measures and then check. Check every measure. Finding one wrong note is easy; finding one wrong note out of thirty-two is not.

### When It Sounds Wrong

| What you hear | What is usually wrong |
| --- | --- |
| A note is too high or too low but the rhythm is right | Wrong octave — drag the note up or down, or use Ctrl/Cmd + up/down arrow |
| The measure has too many or too few beats | Wrong duration. Check for a dot you did or didn't mean to add |
| MuseScore won't let you add a note | The measure is already full, or note input is off |
| Everything shifted over | You inserted instead of replaced — undo and try again |

### Then Make It Sound Like Music

Once the notes are right, add a tempo marking. Around **120 BPM** fits. In MuseScore, find it under **Add → Text → Tempo Marking**, or use the Tempo palette.

## Work Session: Part 3 — Export and Submit

This is graded. You are submitting a **PDF** — I need to see the notation, not just hear it.

### How to Export a PDF from MuseScore

1. Play the whole thing one more time and listen.
2. Open the **File** menu.
3. Choose **Export**.
4. Choose **PDF** as the format.
5. Save it somewhere you can find it.
6. Upload the PDF to the assignment on CTLS. **Upload the file, not a link.**

Save your MuseScore file too. You will not need this one again, but get in the habit now — next week's work builds across several days and losing it will hurt.
