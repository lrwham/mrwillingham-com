## Work Session: Part 1 — Rhythm

Yesterday was about **which** note. Today is about **how long**.

### Note Values

In 4/4 time, one measure holds four beats. Here is what fills it.

| Note | Beats | Looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole note** | 4 | Hollow oval, no stem |
| **Half note** | 2 | Hollow oval with a stem |
| **Quarter note** | 1 | Filled oval with a stem |
| **Eighth note** | ½ | Filled oval with a stem and a flag |

Each one is worth half of the one above it. That is the whole system — it is just repeated halving.

A **dot** after a note adds half of that note's value back on. A dotted quarter note is 1 + ½ = **1½ beats**. You will need that one tomorrow.

### Rests

Every note value has a matching **rest** worth the same amount of time. A rest is not nothing — it is silence you counted on purpose.

### The Time Signature

**4/4** is two separate pieces of information.

- The **top** number says how many beats are in a measure. Four.
- The **bottom** number says which note gets one beat. Four means the quarter note.

Every project we have made this year has been in 4/4. Now you know what the two fours were telling you.

### Counting

Say it out loud with the class.

- Quarter notes: **1 – 2 – 3 – 4**
- Eighth notes: **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

### This Explains Day 9

Remember quantizing? You snapped kick and snare to a **1/4** grid and hi-hats to a **1/8** grid, and the rule was that the grid had to match what you played.

Now you know why. A hi-hat playing eighth notes needs an eighth note grid. Quantize it to 1/4 and every off-beat hi-hat gets dragged onto the beat, which erases half of them. Quantization was never magic. It was always just note values.

## Work Session: Part 2 — Open MuseScore

**MuseScore** is notation software. Soundtrap records and arranges sound. MuseScore writes music down. For the rest of this unit we use both.

Get this far today and you are done.

1. **Launch MuseScore.** If it asks you to sign in, skip it — you do not need an account.
2. Choose **Create New Score**.
3. Set the title to **LastName_First Steps**.
4. Choose **Treble Clef** (the grand staff option is fine too).
5. Set the key signature to **C major** — the one with no sharps and no flats.
6. Set the time signature to **4/4** and the length to **4 measures**. Click **Done**.

### Enter Four Notes

1. Press **N** to turn on **note input mode**. Your cursor changes — that means MuseScore is listening.
2. Click the **quarter note** in the toolbar to choose your duration.
3. Click on the staff to place a note, or just press the letter key **C** on your computer keyboard.
4. Enter four quarter note C's — one full measure.
5. Press **N** again to turn note input off.
6. Press **spacebar** to play it back.

### Three Things to Know Right Now

- **Press N to start and stop entering notes.** Nearly every "MuseScore is doing something weird" problem is note input being on when you thought it was off.
- **Ctrl+Z (Windows) or Cmd+Z (Mac) undoes anything.** Use it freely.
- **Choose the duration first, then place the note.** Not the other way around.
