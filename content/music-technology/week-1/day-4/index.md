## Work Session: Layering and Dynamics

You have heard 20-plus sets now. Some of them were four good loops that still sounded like mud. Here is why.

### Layering

Layering is stacking sounds so that each one occupies its own space. Two loops that live in the same **register** — the same range from low to high — compete for the same space, and the listener hears a smear instead of two parts. Two loops in different registers stack cleanly.

A four-loop set usually works best when the layers split up like this:

| Layer | Register | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drums | Low and high | Set the beat and the energy. |
| Bass | Low | Anchor the harmony under everything else. |
| Harmony | Middle | Fill in the chords — keys, guitar, pads, strings. |
| Melody or FX | High | The part the listener actually follows. |

Two bass loops at once is the most common mistake. So is two busy melodic loops fighting for the top.

### Dynamics

Dynamics means how loud or soft something is. In a DAW you control it with each track's **volume fader**.

Four loops imported at full volume are four loops all shouting at once. The fix is not to make things louder — it is to decide what should be quietest. Pull the supporting layers down until the part you want people to follow is clearly on top.

### Key Vocabulary

Layering
: Stacking multiple sounds so each one occupies its own space in the mix.

Register
: The range a sound sits in, from low to high.

Dynamics
: How loud or soft a sound is, and how that changes over time.

Balance
: The relative volume of each track, set so no layer buries another.

Texture
: How thick or thin the music feels, based on how many layers are playing at once.

### Apply It

Go back to the set you just shared and fix the balance:

1. Pull every track's volume fader all the way down.
2. Bring the drums up first, until they feel solid but not overwhelming.
3. Add the bass, then the harmony, then the melody — one at a time, stopping each one where you can still hear everything under it.
4. Play it through and save.

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
If you keep raising faders to fix a problem, you will run out of room and everything will distort. Lower the layer that is in the way instead.
{{< /callout >}}
