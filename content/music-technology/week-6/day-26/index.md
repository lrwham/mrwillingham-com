## Warmup: Give the Coin a Voice

1. Open Soundtrap → **New Project** → choose the video option.
2. Open the **Video Library** and pick **Urban Runner**.
3. Watch it once. Find the moment the runner touches the first coin.
4. Search the library for a coin sound. Try: *coin, chime, bell, ding, sparkle, glockenspiel, triangle.*
5. Drag it to a track and slide it until it lands **exactly** on the coin.
6. Play it back. If you have to wonder whether it lined up — it didn't. Nudge it and try again.

Save the project as **Urban Runner – Sound Design**. You will use this same project for the whole unit.

> **Note:** Use only sounds you find inside Soundtrap, or files Mr. Willingham posts on CTLS. Do **not** go to freesound.org or any other sound site — they are not approved resources. The Freesound sounds inside Soundtrap's library are fine; they have already been curated.

## Work Session: Ambience, the Sound Nobody Notices

Watch the clip with everything muted. It feels broken, like the video didn't load. Real silence sounds like a mistake, so every scene in film, TV, and games has a quiet background layer running underneath it. That layer is **ambience**. You only notice it when it's gone.

- **Sync point** — a moment on screen a sound must land on exactly.
- **Ambience** — the continuous background bed that makes a scene feel alive.
- **Diegetic / non-diegetic** — the truck engine is in the runner's world. The coin chime isn't. You hear it; the runner never does.

### Build the bed

1. Add one ambience loop that runs under the **entire** clip — city, traffic, crowd, wind, birds.
2. Turn it down until you stop noticing it. Leave it there. That's the right level.
3. Add one more sync point of your choice — a vehicle passing, a jump, a landing.

**Stretch:** swap the ambience so the same clip feels tense instead of fun. Do not change the picture.
