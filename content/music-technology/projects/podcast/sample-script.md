Use this script to practice recording. Read it as if you are the host of *The Sound Zone*. Follow the bracketed directions for expression, and replace **{NAME}** with your own name.

Notice the structure: a hook and introduction, main points in order, a conclusion that signs off, and audio notes in brackets throughout.

## Recording Tips

- Speak about six inches from the microphone.
- Steady, clear voice. Do not rush.
- Follow the `[bracketed directions]` for tone and sound effects.
- It is fine to pause and re-record a section.

## The Script

*[MUSIC: upbeat intro — fade under the first line]*

**Host:** *(Enthusiastic)* "Hey there, science fans! Welcome to The Sound Zone — the podcast where we explore the vibrations that make our world buzz! I'm your host, **{NAME}**. Today we're diving into the fascinating world of sound waves!"

*[Pause for effect]*

**Host:** "Have you ever wondered how your voice travels across a room, or how music reaches your ears? It's all thanks to sound waves. So let's get started."

*[Curious tone]*

**Host:** "First things first — a simple wave has a repeating pattern with a specific frequency and amplitude. Think of a roller coaster: the frequency is how many hills you go over in a certain time, and the amplitude is how high those hills are."

**Host:** "But here's the catch — a sound wave needs a medium to travel through. Air, water, even a solid object. Without a medium, sound has nowhere to go."

*[Higher-pitched voice]*

**Host:** "Imagine shouting in outer space. No one can hear you scream!"

*[Back to normal]*

**Host:** "That's because space is a vacuum — there's no air to carry the sound. Now, how do sound waves move? Something has to disturb the air. Hit a drum with a stick and the drumhead vibrates, moving up and down, creating compressions and rarefactions."

*[SFX: soft whoosh]*

**Host:** "During compression, particles in the medium get pushed together. In rarefaction, they spread apart. That back-and-forth is what lets sound travel."

*[Enthusiastic]*

**Host:** "Want a real-world example? Speakers. Inside a speaker there's a permanent magnet and an electromagnet called the voice coil. The voice coil switches on and off rapidly, so the speaker moves in and out as it's attracted to and repelled by the magnet."

**Host:** "When the speaker moves outward it pushes air molecules together — compression. When it moves inward it pulls them apart — rarefaction. That rapid pushing and pulling creates the sound waves your ears detect. So every time you're jamming to your favorite song, those speakers are busy moving air."

**Host:** "Sound waves can do some tricks, too. Reflection is when a wave bounces off a surface and comes back. Ever shouted and heard your own voice a moment later? That's reflection."

*[SFX: echo effect]*

**Host:** "'Hello... hello... hello...' Pretty neat, right?"

**Host:** "Refraction is when a wave changes direction passing through different materials — it's why sound seems distorted underwater. And diffraction lets sound bend around obstacles and spread out through small openings, which is why you can hear someone talking around a corner."

*[Excited]*

**Host:** "But wait, there's more! Sound can be transmitted through materials. When sound hits a wall, some is absorbed and some passes through to the other side."

*[SFX: distant train]*

**Host:** "Some materials absorb sound on purpose. Music studios put foam on the walls to absorb it."

**Host:** "Now, the speed of sound. In air, about 343 meters per second. In water, 1,481. In iron, 5,120. Can you think of a situation where sound travels through water?"

*[SFX: whale sounds]*

**Host:** "That's right — whales communicate underwater and can hear each other from miles away. And through iron?"

*[SFX: train on tracks]*

*[Faster pace]*

**Host:** "The frequency of a wave determines its pitch. High frequency, high pitch — like a flute. Low frequency, low pitch — like a string bass."

*[SFX: a flute note, then a bass note]*

**Host:** "Amplitude affects how loud it is. Bigger amplitude, louder sound — like ocean waves: the taller the wave, the more energy it has."

*[SFX: a drum hit soft, medium, then loud]*

**Host:** "So the next time you're listening to your favorite song, or hearing a bird chirp, remember all these amazing properties of sound waves at work."

*[MUSIC: closing music — fade in under the last line]*

**Host:** "Thanks for tuning in to The Sound Zone! Keep your ears open and stay curious. Until next time, this is **{NAME}** signing off."

*[End of episode]*
