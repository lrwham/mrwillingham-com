A reusable one-day lesson that flips the melody unit around: harmony first, melody improvised on top. Soundtrap only, no MuseScore. Concepts on [Melody and Form](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#chords-and-harmony).

{{% objectives %}}

## Objectives

- I can explain what a chord is.
- I can set the key and add chords with Soundtrap's Chords tool.
- I can build a chord progression in ABA form, eight measures per section.
- I can improvise a melody over a chord progression.

{{% /objectives %}}

{{% warmup %}}

## Warmup: Find the Chords Tool

1. New project named **ABA Chords**.
2. Add a track with a **keyboard or synth** instrument.
3. Above the instrument, click the **Chords** tab.
4. Explore. Click chords, click the styles on the right, press **Q W E R** and **A S D F**. You cannot break anything.

Official guide: [Soundtrap Support — How to Use the Chord Picker](https://support.soundtrap.com/hc/en-us/articles/18290681373586-How-to-Use-the-Chord-Picker-in-Soundtrap)

The three parts: **chord buttons** (eight squares — a chord is three or four notes at once; click to hear, **+** to add), **playing styles** (the list on the right — Basic holds the chord, Stabs punch it on certain beats, Arpeggiated rolls the notes one at a time), and **key** (bottom bar, next to the tempo — every button changes to match).

## Work Session: Set the Key, Build the Form

**Step 1.** Bottom bar → key → **Am**. The buttons now read **Q** Am · **W** Bdim · **E** C · **R** Dm · **A** E · **S** F · **D** G · **F** Am7. Am is the tonic, home. E is the dominant; it pulls back home.

**Step 2.** Pick a playing style **before** adding chords. Try one from each group: *Full Chord* (Basic), *One and Three* (Stabs), *Dreamer* (Arpeggiated). One style for A; a different one for B if you like.

**Step 3.** One chord per measure.

**A — measures 1–8**

```
Am  |  Am  |  Dm  |  E  |  Am  |  F  |  E  |  Am
```

**B — measures 9–16**

```
C  |  C  |  F  |  G  |  C  |  Am  |  Dm  |  E
```

**A — measures 17–24** (same as the first A)

Why it works: A starts and ends on Am, and **E → Am** at the end is a cadence, the period. B is built around C — same notes, different home, brighter. B ends on E and never comes home, so when A returns it feels like a return.

## Work Session: Add a Beat

New track. Drum loops from the Sound library, or build one in **Drums & Machines → Patterns** ([Beat Making](/music-technology/reference/beat-making/)). Run it under all 24 measures. Keep it simple — kick, snare on 2 and 4, hi-hat. The chords are the star. Optional: thin the drums at the start of B and bring the full beat back for the last A.

## Work Session: Improvise a Melody

One more track with a lead sound — synth, piano, bells. Use only **A · C · D · E · G**, the A minor pentatonic. Every one fits every chord in the progression; there are no wrong notes.

1. Loop the 24 measures and listen once. Hear where the sections change.
2. Play along the second time. Long notes. Leave gaps.
3. Record it live, or draw it into the Piano Roll.
4. End phrases on A, the tonic.
5. Make B different — higher, busier, or more sustained.
