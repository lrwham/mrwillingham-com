Students read music they have never read before, enter it into MuseScore, and find out what it is by playing it back. Prerequisites: [Music Reading 101](/music-technology/reference/music-reading-101/) and the first-launch steps on [MuseScore Basics](/music-technology/reference/musescore-basics/).

## Schedule

| Project Day | Plan |
| ----------- | ---- |
| 1 | **Ode to Joy** — read it first, enter eight measures, export a PDF |
| 2 | **Mystery Melody** — an untitled eight-measure melody; transcribe, play back, title it |
| 3 | **Nine Melodies** — pick one at random from the printed sheet, transcribe, submit a timestamped guess |
| 4 | **Reveal** — last guesses from the recordings, answers and prizes, diagnose a miss |

## Project Day 1: Ode to Joy

Set up: title **Ode to Joy**, composer **Beethoven**, Piano, C major, 4/4, **8** measures, saved as **LastName_OdeToJoy**. Thirty-two beats to fill.

**Read it before touching the computer.** Point at each note and say its name, then the rhythm. Find where the melody steps and where it skips, which measures are identical, and the only notes that are not quarter notes. Measures 1–4 and 5–8 start out the same; only the ending changes.

Every note is a quarter note unless the table says otherwise:

| Measure | Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | E | E | F | G |
| **2** | G | F | E | D |
| **3** | C | C | D | E |
| **4** | E *(dotted quarter)* | D *(eighth)* | D *(half)* | — |
| **5** | E | E | F | G |
| **6** | G | F | E | D |
| **7** | C | C | D | E |
| **8** | D *(dotted quarter)* | C *(eighth)* | C *(half)* | — |

Measures 4 and 8: the dotted quarter takes beat 1 and half of beat 2, the eighth finishes beat 2, the half takes beats 3 and 4.

Enter one measure, play it back, fix it, move on. Add a tempo marking around 120 BPM. Export the **PDF** and upload it ([Turning In Work](/music-technology/reference/turning-in-work/)).

## Project Day 2: Mystery Melody

Same setup, title left **blank**, saved as **LastName_Mystery**. Two rules:

1. **Recognize it early? Keep it to yourself.** Let your table have the same moment.
2. **Do not guess from the notation.** Write what is on the page. Decide too early and you start entering what you remember instead of what is written.

This one has **leaps** — measure 3 jumps up to G and falls a long way down to the C below E — and a lot of **repetition**, where the rhythm does all the work.

| Measure | Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | E | E | E *(half)* | — |
| **2** | E | E | E *(half)* | — |
| **3** | E | G | C *(dotted quarter)* | D *(eighth)* |
| **4** | E *(whole)* | — | — | — |
| **5** | F | F | F | F |
| **6** | F | E | E | E |
| **7** | E | D | D | E |
| **8** | D *(half)* | — | G *(half)* | — |

When it plays back and sounds like a real song: **File → Score Properties** (or double-click the title area), type the title, save, export the PDF. A correct transcription with no title is an incomplete answer.

If it does not sound like anything, something is wrong — usually a wrong octave, sometimes a rhythm that is close but not exact. Set the tempo to about 120; a tune played slowly is much harder to recognize.

## Project Day 3: Nine Melodies

Students get the printed **Mystery Transcription** sheet: nine untitled melodies. **Pick one at random and complete it** — not the easiest-looking one. Enter one measure, play, fix, move on.

Submit each guess in the **guess form** as soon as you have it. Guesses are timestamped; prizes go to the fastest correct guess for each melody, so submit each one right away, and keep answers to yourself — telling someone the answer is handing them your prize. Finished early? Start another. The form stays open overnight for optional extra guesses.

## Project Day 4: Reveal

Warmup: a recording of all nine melodies, in sheet order, plays in the lesson. Write a guess next to every number, including the ones you never transcribed. Nothing is submitted — prizes were decided by the overnight timestamps.

Then all nine go up, one at a time: hear it, call out your guess, see the title. Mark the ones you got. For at least one you missed, find the reason. It is almost always a **wrong octave**, a **rhythm that was close but not exact**, or a **wrong starting note** that put the whole melody in the wrong place. "I just didn't get it" is not a reason.

## Checkpoints

**Day 1**

- [ ] I said every note name out loud before entering anything.
- [ ] Every measure has exactly four beats and it sounds like Ode to Joy.
- [ ] The PDF is uploaded to CTLS.

**Day 2**

- [ ] All eight measures are entered and checked measure by measure.
- [ ] I figured out what it is and the title is on the score.
- [ ] The titled PDF is uploaded.

**Day 3**

- [ ] I picked one melody, entered all of it, and it sounds like a real song.
- [ ] My guess is submitted in the form, and I kept it secret.

**Day 4**

- [ ] A guess is written next to every number on my sheet.
- [ ] For at least one miss, I can say exactly what went wrong.

## Teacher Notes

- The Day 1 melody table is the safety net, not the assignment. Students who read from the notation first get more out of it.
- Day 3's "pick one at random" rule exists because reading all nine to find the easiest burns the period.
- The all-melodies recording and the walkthrough video live in the Day 3 and Day 4 lesson bundles (`all-mysteries.mp3`, `/videos/Mystery-Transcription.mp4`).
- Day 4's second half is the C major scale and a first class melody — the bridge into [Melody and Form](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/).
