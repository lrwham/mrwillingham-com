## Scales

A **scale** is the ordered set of notes a piece is built from, running from one note up to the same note again. Every melody in this unit starts from one.

| Scale | Notes | On the piano |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C major** | C D E F G A B C | All white keys, starting on C |
| **A minor** | A B C D E F G A | All white keys, starting on A |

Same seven notes, completely different sound. The only thing that changed is where home is.

Written as quarter notes, up and down:

| Measures | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| C major | C D E F \| G A B C \| C B A G \| F E D C |
| A minor | A B C D \| E F G A \| A G F E \| D C B A |

**Number them** 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 1 going up and 1 7 6 5 4 3 2 1 coming down (in MuseScore: `cmd + L` under the first note, then space between numbers).

## Tonic and Dominant

Out of seven numbers, two do most of the work.

Tonic — note 1
: **Home.** The note the scale is named after, starts on, and lands back on. Land on it and the music sounds **finished**.

Dominant — note 5
: The note that **points at home**. Land on it and the music sounds like it is about to keep going.

| Scale | Tonic (1) | Dominant (5) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C major | **C** | **G** |
| A minor | **A** | **E** |

You never memorize a dominant. You **count to five**, in any key.

Hear it: play the C scale and stop on G. Hold it — it wants to keep going. Now play G, then C. That is the sound of arriving.

## Cadences

A **cadence** is the end of a phrase — the spot where the music comes to rest. It tells your ear whether the music is pausing or stopping. You only need two:

| End on | Sounds like | Use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant (5) | A **comma** — not done | The halfway point |
| Tonic (1) | A **period** — done | The very end |

Cadences need room. A cadence note that goes by as fast as everything else does not sound like an ending. Give it a **half note or longer**.

### The Ending Formula

**Tonic note + long rhythm.** In A minor: an **A**, held at least a half note, in the last measure. End on any other note and the melody sounds like a question.

## Writing a Melody

- Only notes from the scale.
- Do not copy the scale. Straight up and down is not a melody.
- Mostly **steps** (to the next note), occasional **skips**. Nine leaps in a row is what a mistake looks like.
- Mix rhythms. Eight measures of quarter notes is a metronome, not music.
- Write about four measures, then reuse that material — the same shape starting on a different note, or the same rhythm with different pitches. Repetition is what makes a long melody sound intentional instead of wandering.
- End with the ending formula.

A **question and answer**: the first phrase ends off the tonic (unfinished), the second ends on it (finished).

## Form

**Form** is the map of a piece — the order its sections come in, labeled with letters. The first section is **A**. The next section that sounds different is **B**. If A comes back, it is **A** again, not C, because it is not new.

| Form | Letters | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binary | **A B** | Goes somewhere and stays there. You never get back home. |
| Ternary | **A B A** | Three sections, two ideas. **Departure and return.** |

Ternary is one of the most-used shapes in music because of the return: A sets up the piece, B leaves, and when A comes back it sounds better than the first time because you have been away. Verse, chorus, verse.

**B has to be different, but not too different.** If B sounds exactly like A, nothing left, so nothing comes back. If B sounds like a different piece, the return feels like a mistake. Copy A, then change two or three things: register, rhythm, direction, starting note, motion. See [Copy, Paste, Vary](/music-technology/reference/musescore-basics/#copy-paste-vary).

B's job is to make you want A again, so it ends **unresolved** — on the dominant, not the tonic.

## Chords and Harmony

A **chord** is three or four notes played at once. A **chord progression** is a series of chords, one per measure or so, that a melody sits on top of. In A minor:

- **Am** is the tonic chord — home.
- **E** is the dominant chord — pulls back home.
- **E → Am** is a cadence: the period at the end of a sentence.

## A Minor Pentatonic

**A · C · D · E · G.** Five notes that fit every chord in an A minor progression. Improvise with only these and there are no wrong notes; end phrases on A.

## Key Vocabulary

Scale
: An ordered set of notes a piece is built from.

Step
: Moving to the note right next to it in the scale.

Skip (leap)
: Moving past one or more scale notes.

Tonic
: The home note of a scale (note 1).

Dominant
: Note 5 of a scale; it points at home.

Cadence
: The resting point at the end of a phrase.

Form
: The order of a piece's sections, labeled with letters.

Binary form
: Two sections, A B.

Ternary form
: Three sections, A B A — departure and return.

Repeat sign
: A barline that sends playback back to the start repeat, so a section plays twice.
