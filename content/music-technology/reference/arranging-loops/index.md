Four loops piled on top of each other is a pile. An **arrangement** is the same four loops with decisions about when each one plays and how loud it is.

## Layering

Spread loops out **over time**. Bring them in one at a time, and take one away when things get busy. Two similar loops that fight each other at the same time often work well one after the other — the listener hears the first by itself, then notices the second as a change.

| Measures | What is playing | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | One loop, alone | The listener gets to hear it by itself |
| 3–4 | That loop plus one or two more | Each new loop arrives as a change |
| 5–6 | A fourth loop comes in and one of the earlier three drops out | The new part gets space instead of being piled on |

## Dynamics and Balance

**Dynamics** is how loud or soft something is. In Soundtrap you control it with each track's **volume fader**.

Four loops at full volume are four loops shouting at once. The fix is not to make things louder — it is to decide what is important. The important loop stays at its original volume; pull the supporting loops down until the part you want people to follow is clearly on top.

The reliable method: pull every fader all the way down, then bring loops back up one at a time, stopping each where you can still hear everything under it. If you keep raising faders to fix a problem you will run out of room and everything distorts. Lower the loop that is in the way instead.

## The Four Loop Roles

Every category of loop has a job. A set works when each role is filled clearly.

| Role | Job | If it is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drums / Percussion | Sets the beat and the energy | No pulse |
| Bass | Anchors the harmony at the bottom | Thin and hollow |
| Harmony | Fills in the chords in the middle | Empty between bass and melody |
| Melody / Lead | The part people sing along to | No clear focus |

Four drum loops is one job done four times and three jobs left undone. Not every great piece has exactly one of each, but it is the simplest recipe that works.

## Genre First

Filling every role is not enough. A cinematic string loop, a lo-fi drum loop, a metal bass, and a salsa piano fill all four jobs and still sound like four songs at once.

Loops in the same genre were built with the same assumptions — tempo, instruments, production, feel. Choosing a genre **before** you browse narrows thousands of loops to a few dozen that already agree with each other.

## Key Vocabulary

Layering
: Arranging loops so they build up a cohesive musical idea over time.

Dynamics
: How loud or soft a sound is, and how that changes over time.

Balance
: The relative volume of each track, set so no loop buries another.

Texture
: How thick or thin the music feels, based on how many loops are playing at once.

Cohesive
: Holding together as one piece, so the parts sound like they were meant for each other.

Genre
: A category of music that shares a sound — hip hop, rock, EDM, cinematic, lo-fi.

Arrangement
: Which parts play, when they play, and how they fit together.
