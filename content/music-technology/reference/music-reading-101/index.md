## The Staff

Five lines. Notes sit on the lines and in the spaces between them.

- Notes move in **alphabetical order**: A B C D E F G, then back to A.
- **Low notes are at the bottom.** High notes are at the top.

## The Clef

Five bare lines tell you nothing. A **clef** locks one specific note to one specific line; everything else is counted from there. You are learning two:

| Clef | Also called | Locks down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **G clef** | Treble clef | Where **G** is |
| **F clef** | Bass clef | Where **F** is |

## The Grand Staff

Treble clef on top, bass clef underneath: the **grand staff**, which is what piano music looks like. They meet at **middle C**, which sits on its own short ledger line — one below the treble staff, one above the bass staff. Same note either way.

## The Piano Keyboard

The black keys come in groups of **two** and **three**, repeating forever. Memorize four landmarks:

| Note | How to find it |
| --- | --- |
| **D** | In "duh" middle of the pair of black keys |
| **E** | Just right of the group of two |
| **F** | In "front" of the group of three |
| **B** | "Behind" the group of three |

### Octaves

Dozens of keys, only **seven note names**, so the names repeat every eight notes. The distance from one C to the next C is an **octave**; both keys are C, one sounds higher.

### Whole Steps and Half Steps

- A **half step** is the distance to the very next key, black keys included.
- A **whole step** has exactly one key in between.

Only two pairs of neighboring white keys are a half step apart: **B–C** and **E–F** (no black key between them). Every other white-key pair is a whole step. Those two exceptions are why scales work the way they do.

## Note Values

In 4/4, one measure holds four beats. Each value is half of the one above it.

| Note | Beats | Looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole note** | 4 | Hollow oval, no stem |
| **Half note** | 2 | Hollow oval with a stem |
| **Quarter note** | 1 | Filled oval with a stem |
| **Eighth note** | ½ | Filled oval with a stem and a flag |
| **Sixteenth note** | ¼ | Two flags |

A **dot** adds half the note's value back on. A dotted quarter is 1 + ½ = **1½ beats**.

## Rests

Every note value has a matching **rest** worth the same amount of time. A rest is not nothing — it is silence you counted on purpose.

## The Time Signature

**4/4** is two pieces of information. The **top** number is how many beats are in a measure (four). The **bottom** number is which note gets one beat (four means the quarter note).

## Counting

- Quarter notes: **1 – 2 – 3 – 4**
- Eighth notes: **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**

This is also why quantizing works the way it does: a hi-hat playing eighth notes needs a 1/8 grid. See [Beat Making](/music-technology/reference/beat-making/#quantization).
