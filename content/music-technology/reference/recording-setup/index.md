## How Recording Works

- A **microphone** turns sound waves into an electric signal.
- **Analog** recording stores the whole wave as a magnetic pattern or a groove.
- **Digital** recording uses an **analog-to-digital converter (ADC)** to measure the wave thousands of times a second and store it as 1s and 0s. In our room, the **audio interface** is the ADC. That is why a digital recording can be copied forever without getting worse.
- **Sampling frequency** is samples per second; **sampling precision** is how many numbers describe each sample's height.

## What Makes a Clean Take

- **Quiet room.** The mic records everything, including the table next to you. Only one mic per table, and you are silent when it is not your turn.
- **Distance.** About six inches. Too far is thin; too close is boomy.
- **Headroom.** Set the gain so the meter moves but never hits red. Red is **clipping**, and a clipped take cannot be fixed.

Gain
: How much the interface boosts the mic. The small knob on the interface.

Headroom
: The space between your loudest peak and red.

Set gain for the **loudest** sound you plan to make, then leave it alone.

## Set Up

1. Metal desktop stand flat on the table.
2. Mount the mic on the stand.
3. **XLR cable** from the mic to an input on the **audio interface**. Push until it clicks.
4. Interface **USB** to the computer. Gain knob all the way **down**.
5. In Soundtrap, open or create the project the lesson names. Add a **Voice & Mic** track and set its input to the interface.
6. Say "check." Turn the gain up until the meter moves — into the yellow, never red.

Meter not moving? Check both XLR ends, that the gain is on the right input, and the Soundtrap input setting.

## The Take Protocol

Every take: say "quiet, recording" → one second of silence → the sound or the line → one second of silence → stop. Listen back with headphones. Bad take? Delete it and go again.

For a script: record **one section at a time**, not the whole thing in one take. If you make a mistake, do not stop — be silent for three seconds, then re-read the line. The silence is where you will cut later.

## Tear Down

1. Save.
2. Turn every gain knob on the interface back to **zero**.
3. Unplug the XLR — press the tab and pull the connector, never the cable.
4. Take the mic off the stand.
5. Coil the cable in loose loops. Everything back where it came from.
