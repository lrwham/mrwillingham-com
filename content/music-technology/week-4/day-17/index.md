## Objectives

- I can identify a melody by ear from a recording.
- I can compare my transcription to the real thing and find where I was off.
- I can write the C major scale in MuseScore.
- I can use the notes of the C major scale to write a short melody.

## Warmup: Last Guesses

All nine melodies, in sheet order. Listen to as many as you want, as many times as you want, and write a guess next to **every** number — including the ones you never transcribed. Nothing is submitted; prizes were decided by the timestamps.

## Work Session: The Reveal

Project Day 4 of [Mystery Transcription](/music-technology/projects/mystery-transcription/#project-day-4-reveal). One at a time: hear it, call out your guess, see the title. Mark the ones you got. For at least one you missed, find the reason — a wrong octave, a rhythm that was close but not exact, or a wrong starting note. "I just didn't get it" is not a reason.

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Reveal

- [ ] For at least one miss, I can say exactly what went wrong.

## Work Session: The C Major Scale and a First Melody

You are done reading other people's music. Follow along — one step at a time, together.

New score: Piano, C major, 4/4, 4 measures, saved as **LastName_CMajor**. Enter the C major scale as quarter notes — C D E F G A B C — and play it back ([Melody and Form](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#scales)).

Then we write a first melody together, on the board and on your screen. One rule: every note comes from the scale. Mostly steps, the occasional skip.

Turn in the melody as an MP3 and a PDF ([Turning In Work](/music-technology/reference/turning-in-work/)).

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Scale and Melody

- [ ] The C major scale is entered and plays back correctly.
- [ ] The class melody is on my screen, saved as LastName_CMajor.
- [ ] MP3 and PDF uploaded to CTLS.

{{% /checkpoint %}}
