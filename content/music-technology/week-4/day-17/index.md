## Work Session: Part 1 — The Reveal

We go through all nine together, one at a time. You hear it, you call out your guess, then the title goes up.

Prizes go to the **fastest correct guess** for each melody. The form timestamped every submission, so the winner is whoever got it in first — not whoever is loudest right now.

As each answer comes up, check it against your sheet:

- Mark the ones you got.
- For at least one you missed, go back to what you wrote and find the reason. It is almost always one of three things: a **wrong octave**, a **rhythm that was close but not exact**, or a **wrong starting note** that put the whole melody in the wrong place.

Knowing which one it was is the part that makes you better at this. "I just didn't get it" is not a reason.

## Work Session: Part 2 — The C Major Scale

You are done reading other people's music. From here on you write your own, and a **scale** is where every melody starts.

Follow along with Mr. Willingham. Do not run ahead — we are doing this one step at a time, together.

### Set Up Your Score

1. **Create New Score** in MuseScore.
2. Instrument: **Piano**.
3. Key: **C major**. Time signature: **4/4**. Measures: **4**.
4. Save as **LastName_CMajor**.

### Write the Scale

The C major scale is eight notes: **C D E F G A B C**. It starts on C, walks straight up to the next C, and uses no sharps and no flats — on a piano it is all white keys.

Enter it as **quarter notes**, then play it back. It should sound like the thing you have heard a thousand times without knowing its name. If it does not, one of your notes skipped a step.

### Key Vocabulary

Scale
: An ordered set of notes that a piece of music is built from, running from one note up to the same note again.

C Major Scale
: The eight notes C D E F G A B C — no sharps, no flats.

Step
: Moving from one note to the note right next to it in the scale. A melody that moves by step is easy to sing and easy to read.

### Write Our First Melody

Now we write one together, on the board and on your screen at the same time.

Every note comes from the scale you just entered. That is the only rule. Stay inside C D E F G A B C and it will sound like it belongs together — which is exactly why we learned the scale first.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
Mostly steps, with the occasional skip, is what a singable melody looks like. Nine leaps in a row is what a mistake looks like.
{{< /callout >}}
