## Work Session: Part 1 — Form

**Form** is the map of a piece — the order its sections come in. Musicians label those sections with **letters**.

- The first section is **A**.
- The next section that sounds different is **B**.
- If A comes back, you write **A** again — not C, because it is not new.

That is the whole system, and it describes almost any song you have ever heard.

| Form | Letters | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binary | **A B** | Goes somewhere and stays there. You never get back home. |
| Ternary | **A B A** | Three sections, two ideas. **Departure and return.** |

Ternary is one of the most-used shapes in music because of that return. A sets up the piece, B leaves, and when A comes back it sounds better than it did the first time — because you have been away. You already know the shape from songs: verse, chorus, verse.

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
**B has to be different — but not too different.** If B sounds exactly like A, nothing left, so nothing comes back. If B sounds like a completely different piece, the return feels like a mistake. B should be recognizably *the same piece* doing something *noticeably different*.
{{< /callout >}}

### Key Vocabulary

Form
: The map of a piece — the order its sections come in, labeled with letters.

Binary Form
: Two sections, A B.

Ternary Form
: Three sections, A B A — departure and return.

## Work Session: Part 2 — New Score, Write A

You are building one piece in **both** forms today — binary first, then ternary — and mostly with copy and paste.

**Create New Score:** Piano, key of **C major**, **4/4**, **8** measures. Title: your original made-up name. Composer: your name. Save under that name.

### Measures 1–8

- Only five notes: **C, D, E, G, A**. Four beats in every measure.
- **Two four-measure phrases** — a question and an answer.
- The **question** (m1–4) ends **off** C. It should sound unfinished.
- The **answer** (m5–8) ends **on** C, half note or longer.

Play it back now. Everything else today is a copy of these eight measures, so a mistake in here gets copied twice.

## Work Session: Part 3 — Copy, Paste, Vary: Section B

Composers do not write B from a blank page. You **copy A and change it** — that is why B still sounds like the same piece.

1. Add **8 measures**: **Add → Measures → Insert Measures at End of Score**.
2. Click measure **1**, shift-click measure **8**, **copy**.
3. Click measure **9**, **paste**.

Play all 16. It is boring on purpose — that is **A A**, not A B. Nothing left home, so nothing came back.

### Change At Least Two Things in Measures 9–16

- **Register** — select B and press **Ctrl/Cmd + up arrow** for a whole octave. Easiest contrast there is.
- **Rhythm** — if A is mostly quarters, use halves or eighths.
- **Direction** — if A climbs, make B fall.
- **Starting note** — begin on G or A instead of C.
- **Motion** — if A moves by step, put a couple of leaps in B.

**Two is enough. Three is plenty.** Change all five and it stops being a B section — it becomes a different piece, and the return of A sounds like a mistake instead of a homecoming.

What does *not* change: still only C, D, E, G, A, still two four-measure phrases, still four beats per measure.

### End B on G

B's job is to make you want A again, so it has to end **unresolved** — not on C. If B ends comfortably on C, the piece already sounds over. End it on **G**.

Play measures 1–16. **That is binary form — A B.** It stops, but it does not really finish.

## Work Session: Part 4 — Paste A Back: Ternary

Turning binary into ternary takes one paste.

1. Add **8 more measures** — you should have **24**.
2. Select measures **1–8**, **copy**.
3. Click measure **17**, **paste**.

Play all 24. You should hear the shape: **familiar, then away, then home.**

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
**Do this back to back — it is the point of the whole lesson.** Play measures 1–16 and stop: that is **binary**. Now play 1–24: that is **ternary**. Same notes, same B section. One paste is the only difference, and only one of them sounds finished.
{{< /callout >}}
