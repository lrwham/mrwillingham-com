## Objectives

- I can label sections of a piece with letters.
- I can explain the difference between binary and ternary form.
- I can build a B section by copying my A section and changing it.

{{% /objectives %}}

{{% warmup %}}

## Warmup: Watch, Then Talk It Out

Short video on the TV. Screens down, headphones off. Have an answer ready: what letter does the first section get, and the next different one? Write binary in letters, then ternary. In A B A, why is the third section A and not C? Name a song where a part you already heard comes back at the end.

### Checkpoint: Warmup

- [ ] I watched with my screen down and have an answer for all four questions.

{{% /warmup %}}

{{% worksession %}}

## Work Session: Form, Then Build Both

[Form](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#form): A, then B, then A again if it comes back. Binary goes somewhere and stays; ternary leaves and returns.

Then Project Day 2 of the [Melody Project](/music-technology/projects/melody-project/#project-day-2-binary-and-ternary):

1. **Section A** — new score, C major, 8 measures, original title. Only C, D, E, G, A. A question phrase that ends off C and an answer that ends on C. Fix mistakes here first; everything else is a copy.
2. **Section B** — [copy, paste, vary](/music-technology/reference/musescore-basics/#copy-paste-vary) into measures 9–16. Change at least two things. End B on **G**. Play 1–16: binary.
3. **Ternary** — add eight measures, paste A into 17–24. Play 1–16 and stop, then 1–24. Same notes, one paste, only one sounds finished.

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Work Session

- [ ] A uses only C, D, E, G, A; question ends off C, answer on C.
- [ ] B is a varied copy, still those five notes, ending on G.
- [ ] 24 measures — A, B, A — played back to back against the binary version.
- [ ] Saved under my original title.

{{% /checkpoint %}}
