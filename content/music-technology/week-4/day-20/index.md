## Work Session: Part 1 — Two Scales, Numbered

New score. Piano, **4/4**, key of **C major**, **8** measures. Title it **Scales**. This is scratch work — it is not your composition.

All quarter notes:

| Measures | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| 1–4, C major | C D E F \| G A B C \| C B A G \| F E D C |
| 5–8, A minor | A B C D \| E F G A \| A G F E \| D C B A |

Play both. Same seven white keys, completely different sound. The only thing that changed is where each one starts and ends.

### Number Them

Click the first note, press **Ctrl+L** / **Cmd+L**, and type **1** — then **space**, **2**, **space**, and so on. Number both scales **1 2 3 4 5 6 7 1** going up and **1 7 6 5 4 3 2 1** coming down.

## Work Session: Part 2 — Tonic, Dominant, and Cadence

Out of seven numbers, two do most of the work.

### Key Vocabulary

Tonic — note 1
: **Home.** The note the scale is named after. Land on it and the music sounds **finished**.

Dominant — note 5
: The note that **points at home**. Land on it and the music sounds like it is about to keep going.

Cadence
: The end of a musical phrase — the spot where the music comes to rest. It tells your ear whether the music is **pausing** or **stopping**.

| Scale | Tonic (1) | Dominant (5) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C major | **C** | **G** |
| A minor | **A** | **E** |

You never memorize a dominant. You **count to five**, and that works in every key there is.

**Hear it:** play your C scale and stop on **G**. Hold it — it wants to keep going. Now play **G**, then **C**. That is the sound of arriving.

Cadences are punctuation, and you only need two:

| End on | Sounds like | Use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant (5) | A **comma** — not done | Halfway point |
| Tonic (1) | A **period** — done | The very end |

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
**Cadences need room.** A cadence note that goes by as fast as everything else does not sound like an ending. Give it a **half note or longer**.
{{< /callout >}}

## Work Session: Part 3 — Write Section A

New score — **this one is the real one.** Piano, 4/4, key of C major, **8** measures. Title: your original made-up name. Composer: your name. Save.

### Pick Your Home

Your five notes are **C, D, E, G, A** — and those same five notes work in both keys. So pick one, because it decides your cadence notes:

| Home | Tonic | Dominant | Sounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C major | **C** | **G** | Bright |
| A minor | **A** | **E** | Darker |

### Measures 1–8

- Only **C, D, E, G, A**. Four beats in every measure.
- Two four-measure phrases — a question and an answer.
- Mix your rhythms. Eight measures of quarter notes is a metronome, not music.
- **Measure 4** ends on anything *except* your tonic.
- **Measure 8** ends on your **dominant**, half note or longer. This is the halfway point of the piece — the comma.

Play it back. It should feel like the music leaned forward and waited.

## Work Session: Part 4 — Copy, Paste, Vary: Section B

You do not write B from a blank page. You copy A and change it — that is why B still sounds like the same piece.

1. Add **8 measures** at the end: **Add → Measures → Insert Measures at End of Score**.
2. Select measures **1–8** and **copy**.
3. Click measure **9** and **paste**.

Play it. Right now it is A A, and it is supposed to sound boring.

### Change At Least Two Things in Measures 9–16

- **Register** — select it and press **Ctrl/Cmd + up arrow** for a whole octave.
- **Rhythm** — if A is mostly quarters, use halves or eighths.
- **Direction** — if A climbs, make B fall.
- **Starting note** — begin somewhere A did not.

Two is enough, three is plenty. Change everything and it stops being a B section — it becomes a different piece.

### Measure 16 — the Period

End measure 16 on your **tonic**, on a **whole note**. Longest note in the piece, and the last one.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
Measure 16 is a copy of measure 8, so it is sitting on your dominant right now. Play measures 1–16, **then** change that one note to your tonic and play it again. One note is the whole difference between a comma and a period.
{{< /callout >}}

**You now have a piece in binary form — A B.** Save it.

## Closing

Three words: **tonic** is home, **dominant** points at home, and a **cadence** is what happens when a phrase lands on one of them. Comma in the middle, period at the end.
