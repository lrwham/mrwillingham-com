## Work Session: The A Minor Scale and the Tonic

Project Day 1 of the [Melody Project](/music-technology/projects/melody-project/#project-day-1-a-minor-and-the-tonic). New score: Piano, **A minor**, 4/4, **12** measures, an original title, your name.

Measures 1–4: the A minor scale up and down ([Melody and Form](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#scales)). Same white keys as yesterday, and it should not sound like yesterday at all. The thing that changed is the **tonic** — home. Play the scale and stop on G to hear why.

## Work Session: Your Eight-Measure Melody

Measures 5–12 are yours. The rules are on [Writing a Melody](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#writing-a-melody): only scale notes, do not copy the scale, mix rhythms, mostly steps, reuse your material, and **end on A, half note or longer** — the ending formula.

Turn in: save under your title, export MP3 and PDF, attach both in CTLS.

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Melody

- [ ] Measures 5–12 full, only A minor notes, more than one rhythm.
- [ ] Last note is an A, half note or longer.
- [ ] MP3 and PDF both in CTLS — today's and yesterday's.

{{% /checkpoint %}}

{{% /worksession %}}

{{% closing %}}

## Closing

Same seven notes as yesterday; only home moved. That is most of what composing is. Your ending is two ideas — the tonic and a long note — and every melody this unit uses it.

Closing question: change your last A to a B and leave everything else alone. Why does that one change undo the ending?

{{% /closing %}}
