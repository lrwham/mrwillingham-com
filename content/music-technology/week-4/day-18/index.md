## Warmup: How We Turn Work In

Every MuseScore assignment in this class gets turned in the same way. Learn it once today and you never have to think about it again.

### 1. Original name

Not *Untitled Score*, not your last name. You make up a title nobody else would land on by accident: **Purple Car. Giant Fork. Toasted Pop Tarts Are Fire.**

It goes in two places and they match: the **title box on the score** and the **file name**. Your name goes in the composer spot.

### 2. Two exports

Saving is not turning in — the **.mscz** file stays with you so you can keep working. What gets submitted is two exports of it:

| File | How | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MP3 | File → Export → MP3 | What your music sounds like |
| PDF | File → Export → PDF | What it looks like on the page |

Both exports carry the project's original name: **Purple Car.mp3** and **Purple Car.pdf**.

### 3. Both files attached in CTLS

Two files, one submission. One file is an *incomplete* assignment, not a late one — I cannot grade the notes without the PDF or the sound without the MP3.

## Work Session: Part 1 — The A Minor Scale

Yesterday you built a melody out of C major. Today you get the same seven notes back and a completely different sound out of them.

**Create New Score:** Piano, key of **A minor** (no sharps or flats — same key signature as C major), **4/4**, **12** measures. Title: your original made-up name. Composer: your name. Save under that name.

### Measures 1–4: The Scale, Up and Down

All quarter notes:

| Measure 1 | Measure 2 | Measure 3 | Measure 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A B C D | E F G A | A G F E | D C B A |

Play it back. All white keys — the exact same notes as yesterday, and it should not sound like yesterday at all. Something changed, and it was not the notes.

## Work Session: Part 2 — The Tonic

Here is what changed. Every scale has one note that is **home**.

### Key Vocabulary

Tonic
: The home note of a scale — the note it is named after, starts on, and lands back on. In C major the tonic is **C**. In A minor it is **A**.

C major and A minor use identical notes. The only thing that moved was the tonic, and that alone changes the mood of the whole scale — your ear picks a home note and hears everything else in relation to it.

### End on the Tonic, and Land Long

Play the A minor scale but stop on G, one note short. It feels unfinished, like a sentence that just stops in the middle of

That is the pull of home. End on any other note and the melody sounds like a question; end on the tonic and it sounds **finished**.

The note is only half of it. A last note that goes by as fast as everything before it sounds like you ran out of measures, not like an ending. Give it room.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
**The ending formula:** tonic note + long rhythm. In A minor: an **A**, held at least a half note, in your last measure.
{{< /callout >}}

## Work Session: Part 3 — Your Eight-Measure Melody

Measures **5–12** are yours — twice as long as yesterday's melody, and long enough that it has to actually go somewhere.

- **Only notes from the scale.** A B C D E F G. Nothing borrowed.
- **Do not copy the scale.** Straight up and down is not a melody.
- **Mix your rhythms.** Eight measures of quarter notes is a metronome, not music.
- **Eight measures exactly** — 5 through 12, all full.
- **End on A, held long.** Half note or whole note in measure 12.
- **Mostly steps, occasional skips** — a melody you could sing.

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
**Eight measures is long.** Do not write eight measures of unrelated ideas. Write about four, then use that material again — the same shape starting on a different note, or the same rhythm with different pitches. Repetition is what makes a long melody sound intentional instead of wandering.
{{< /callout >}}

Play it back often. If a spot sounds wrong, it probably is — fix it while it is four notes instead of forty.

### Turning It In

Save under your original title → export **MP3** → export **PDF** → attach **both** in CTLS.

## Closing

Eight measures today out of the same seven notes you used yesterday. Nothing about the raw material changed — only where home was. That is most of what composing actually is: a small set of notes and decisions about where they point.

You also have an ending that works, and it is two ideas: **the tonic** and **a long note**. Every melody you write this unit will use it.

### Closing Question

Your melody ends on A and sounds finished. What would happen if you changed that last note to a B and left everything else alone — and why would that one change undo the ending?
