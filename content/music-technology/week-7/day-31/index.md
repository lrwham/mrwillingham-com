## Work Session: Set Up, Test, Tear Down

### From the movie

- A **microphone** turns sound waves into an electric signal.
- **Analog** stores the whole wave as a magnetic pattern or a groove.
- **Digital** uses an **analog-to-digital converter (ADC)** to measure the wave thousands of times a second and store it as 1s and 0s. In our room, the **audio interface** is the ADC.
- **Sampling frequency** = samples per second. **Sampling precision** = how many numbers describe each sample's height.

### What makes a clean take

- **Quiet room.** The mic records everything.
- **Distance.** Too far is thin; too close is boomy.
- **Headroom.** Set the **gain knob** so the meter moves but never hits red. Red is **clipping**, and a clipped take can't be fixed.

### Step 1: Set up

1. Metal desktop stand flat on the table.
2. Mount the mic on the stand.
3. **XLR cable** from the mic to an input on the **audio interface**. Push until it clicks.
4. Interface **USB** to the computer. Gain knob starts all the way down.
5. In Soundtrap, start a project named **Found Sounds**. Add a **Voice & Mic** track and set the input to the interface.
6. Say "check." Turn the gain up until the meter moves — not into red.

Meter not moving? Check both XLR ends, the gain on the right input, and the Soundtrap input setting.

### Step 2: Test and record

Record several takes of each:

- **Paper** — rip slowly, rip fast, crumple.
- **Metal stand base** — fingernail, knuckle, pencil.

Every take: "Quiet, recording" → one second of silence → the sound → one second of silence → stop. Listen back with headphones. Bad take? Delete it and go again.

### Step 3: Tear down

1. Save.
2. Turn every gain knob on the interface back to zero.
3. Unplug the XLR — press the tab and pull the connector, never the cable.
4. Take the mic off the stand.
5. Coil the cable in loose loops and put everything back where it came from.
