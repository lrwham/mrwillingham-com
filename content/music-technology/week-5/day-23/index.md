## Warmup: Find the Chords Tool

Soundtrap today. No MuseScore.

1. Start a **new project**. Name it **ABA Chords**.
2. Add a track with a **keyboard or synth** instrument.
3. Above the instrument, click the **Chords** tab.
4. Explore. Click chords. Click the styles on the right. Press **Q W E R** and **A S D F**. No rules — you cannot break anything.

Official guide: [Soundtrap Support — How to Use the Chord Picker](https://support.soundtrap.com/hc/en-us/articles/18290681373586-How-to-Use-the-Chord-Picker-in-Soundtrap)

### The Three Parts

- **Chord buttons** — the eight squares. A **chord** is three or four notes played at once. Click to hear it, click **+** to add it to your track.
- **Playing styles** — the list on the right. This is the *rhythm* the chord plays with, grouped in three kinds:
    - **Basic** holds the chord out — *Full Chord*, *On One*.
    - **Stabs** punch it short on certain beats — *On Air*, *One and Three*, *All Four*.
    - **Arpeggiated** rolls the notes one at a time — *Layout*, *Dreamer*, *Simple Stride*, *Storytime*.
- **Key** — bottom bar, next to the tempo. Every chord button changes to match the key.

## Work Session 1: Set the Key, Build the Form

### Step 1: Change the key to Am

Bottom bar → key → **Am**. Your buttons now read:

**Q** Am · **W** Bdim · **E** C · **R** Dm · **A** E · **S** F · **D** G · **F** Am7

- **Am** is the **tonic** — home.
- **E** is the **dominant** — it pulls back home.

### Step 2: Pick a playing style

Click a style on the right **before** you add chords — it sets the rhythm. Try one from each group: *Full Chord* (Basic), *One and Three* (Stabs), *Dreamer* (Arpeggiated). Use one style for A. If you want, use a different one for B.

### Step 3: Add one chord per measure

**A — measures 1–8**

```
Am  |  Am  |  Dm  |  E  |  Am  |  F  |  E  |  Am
```

**B — measures 9–16**

```
C  |  C  |  F  |  G  |  C  |  Am  |  Dm  |  E
```

**A — measures 17–24** (same as the first A)

```
Am  |  Am  |  Dm  |  E  |  Am  |  F  |  E  |  Am
```

### Why it works

- **A starts and ends on Am** — home. The last two chords, **E → Am**, are a **cadence**: the musical period at the end of a sentence.
- **B is built around C** instead of Am. Same notes, different home, brighter sound. That is the contrast.
- **B ends on E** and never comes home — so when A returns, it *feels* like a return.

## Work Session 2: Add a Beat

1. Add a new track.
2. Use **drum loops** from the Sound library, or build one in **Drums & Machines → Patterns**.
3. Run it under all **24 measures**.

Keep it simple — kick, snare on 2 and 4, hi-hat. The chords are the star.

**Optional:** thin the drums out at the start of B, then bring the full beat back for the last A.

## Work Session 3: Improvise a Melody

Add one more track with a lead sound — synth, piano, bells.

### Use only these five notes

**A · C · D · E · G**

That is **A minor pentatonic**. Every one of those notes fits every chord in your progression. There are no wrong notes.

### How

1. **Loop the 24 measures and listen once.** Hear where the sections change.
2. **Play along the second time.** Long notes. Leave gaps.
3. **Record it** — live, or draw it into the Piano Roll.
4. **End phrases on A** — the tonic. It sounds finished.
5. **Make B different** — higher, busier, or more sustained.
