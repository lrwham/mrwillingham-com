## Work Session: Five Minutes to Export and Submit

You get **five minutes**. This is not time to write new music — it is time to export what you have and get it turned in, because in five minutes we start listening.

1. **Check the top of your score.** Three things have to be there: your **original title**, the subtitle **Binary/Ternary Form Melody Project**, and your **name**.
2. **Export the MP3.** **File → Export**, choose **MP3**, and save it under **your title** — not your last name, not "Untitled score." This is the file we hear.
3. **Export the PDF.** **File → Export** again, choose **PDF**, same title, same spelling. This is the file I read.
4. **Upload both to CTLS.** Two files, one assignment. **Files, not links.**

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
**Important:** A PDF with no MP3 is half an assignment, and so is an MP3 with no PDF. When the five minutes are up we stop and start listening — whatever is uploaded is what gets played.
{{< /callout >}}

## Work Session: Listening Party

**Log off.** Screens down, headphones off. Every project plays out loud on the big speakers, start to finish.

You wrote this piece with your ears three inches from a laptop. It is a different piece in a room. Some of what you hear today is your own music sounding better than you thought it did.

While each one plays, listen for three things:

1. The moment the **section changes**. Can you hear where B starts without being told?
2. The **bass line** — where it leans on the **dominant**, and where it comes home to the **tonic**.
3. **One thing the composer did that you did not think of.** Be specific. "It was good" is not an answer.

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
**Important:** Every piece gets the same room — quiet while it plays, and no commentary about whose it is. It takes something to put your own music in front of thirty people.
{{< /callout >}}
