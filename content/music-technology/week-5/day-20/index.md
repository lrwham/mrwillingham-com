## Work Session: Two Scales, Numbered

Scratch score titled **Scales**: C major in measures 1–4, A minor in 5–8, both numbered — the tables and the `cmd + L` trick are on [Melody and Form](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#scales).

Then [Tonic and Dominant](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#tonic-and-dominant) and [Cadences](/music-technology/reference/melody-and-form/#cadences): note 1 is home, note 5 points at home, and a cadence on each is a period or a comma. Play 5 then 1 and hear the pull.

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Scales

- [ ] I can name the tonic and dominant of C major and A minor.

## Work Session: A Binary Piece with Cadences

Project Day 3 of the [Melody Project](/music-technology/projects/melody-project/#project-day-3-tonic-dominant-and-cadence). The real score: C major key signature, 8 measures, original title. Pick a home (C major or A minor) — same five notes, C D E G A, either way.

**Section A:** two phrases; measure 4 ends off the tonic; **measure 8 ends on the dominant, held long** — the comma.

**Section B:** copy, paste, vary into 9–16; **measure 16 ends on the tonic, whole note** — the period. Play it on the dominant first, then change that one note.

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Binary Piece

- [ ] Measures 1–8 use only C, D, E, G, A; measure 8 on the dominant, held.
- [ ] B is a varied copy; measure 16 on the tonic, whole note.
- [ ] Saved under my original title.

{{% /checkpoint %}}

## Closing

Tonic is home, dominant points at home, a cadence lands on one of them. Nothing is submitted today. Tomorrow you add a repeat sign and a bass line and turn it in for a summative grade.

Closing question: measures 8 and 16 are nearly the same music. What would happen if you swapped their last notes?
