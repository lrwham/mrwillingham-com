Engineers do not guess their way to a solution. They follow a process — the same six steps, every project, whether the product is an ankle brace, a paper skimmer, or a therapeutic toy. You followed all six during the AFO challenge before anyone showed you the list. Here is the list.

## The six steps, in order

1. **Define the problem.** Read the design brief. Who is the client? What do they need? What are the criteria (what a good solution must do) and the constraints (the limits you have to work inside — time, materials, size, cost)?
2. **Generate concepts.** Brainstorm. Lots of ideas, sketched quickly, no judging yet. Bad ideas are allowed — they lead to good ones.
3. **Develop a solution.** Pick the strongest concept and work it out in detail: sketches with dimensions, materials, how the parts fit together.
4. **Construct and test a prototype.** Build it. Then test it against the criteria from step 1 — not against how good it looks.
5. **Evaluate the solution.** What worked? What did not? What does the data say? Decide what to change.
6. **Present the solution.** Show the client. Explain what you built, why, and how well it met the requirements.

The process loops. A test in step 4 that fails sends you back to step 3, or even step 2. That is not a mistake — that is the process working.

## What each step looked like in the AFO challenge

| Step | What your team did |
| --- | --- |
| Define the problem | Read the design brief: a child with cerebral palsy needs an ankle-foot orthosis that supports the ankle and still lets the foot move. |
| Generate concepts | Brainstormed and sketched ideas in Notebook Entry #1 before touching any materials. |
| Develop a solution | Picked one design and decided which recycled materials would do each job. |
| Construct and test a prototype | Built it from recycled materials and tested it on a real ankle. |
| Evaluate the solution | Compared it to the design brief and to real AFO designs; scored the class prototypes in a decision matrix. |
| Present the solution | Shared the prototype with the class from your table. |

## Tools engineers use along the way

Design Brief
: A short document from the client that states the problem, the design statement, and the design requirements. Everything you build gets judged against it.

Prototype
: A first working model of a solution, built to test an idea — not a finished product.

Decision Matrix
: A table that scores several solutions against the same criteria so the choice is based on evidence instead of opinion. Total each row; the highest total wins — even when it did not win every column.

Engineering Notebook
: The dated, headed record of everything you did: the brief, the sketches, the measurements, the test data, and what you decided. If it is not in the notebook, it did not happen.
