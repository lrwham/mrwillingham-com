## Warmup: Open the Class Data

The class spreadsheet is linked in CTLS. These are the statistics already calculated on it — all 147 launches from both periods, in feet:

| min | median | mean | mode | max | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | IQR | range | count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.0 | 12.5 | 12.9 | 13.0 | 33.0 | 7.3 | 12.5 | 18.5 | 11.3 | 32.0 | 147 |

Look it over quietly and be ready to answer:

1. How many launches are on this sheet? How is that different from the three trials you ran?
2. The mean is 12.9 ft and the median is 12.5 ft. What does it tell you that those two numbers are so close together?
3. The shortest launch was 1.0 ft and the longest was 33.0 ft — a range of 32 ft. Does 32 ft describe a typical launch? Why or why not?
4. Five of these statistics are the ones you need to draw a box plot. Which five?

### Ruler check

In a few minutes you plot 7.3 and 12.5 on a number line, and neither lands on a grid line. Reading a ruler and reading a number line are the same skill, so we sharpen it first.

1. Line the object up with the **zero mark** — not the end of the ruler. There is usually a little blank space before zero.
2. Count the small marks. Each inch is cut into 16 equal spaces, so one small mark is 1/16 in.
3. If the object does not start at zero, you must **subtract**: length = right reading − left reading.

Quick check with your neighbor: an object's left edge sits on the 1/2 in. mark and its right edge sits on the 2 in. mark. How long is it? (If you said 2 in., re-read rule 3.)

Then open the **Reading a Ruler** practice form linked in CTLS and answer all four questions. Use a real ruler from your table caddy if it helps.

## Work Session: Draw the Plot Together

### Set up the page

1. Next blank page in your Engineering Notebook. Head it: **1.4 Skimmer Statistics — Class Box Plot — 8/24/2026**.
2. Under the heading, copy the five-number summary from the table above, each on its own line, in feet with one decimal.
3. Take a half sheet of graph paper and tape it in below your five numbers. **Tape the top edge only,** so the page still closes flat.
4. Ruler and pencil. Pencil first — pen only for labels at the end.

### Draw it

Follow along with Mr. Willingham. Do not run ahead — every step gets checked.

1. **Number line.** Along the bottom, 1 square = 1 foot, scaled 0 to 35. Label every 5 feet. Title the axis **Distance (feet)**.
2. **Mark the five numbers** as small ticks above the line: minimum 1.0, Q1 7.3, median 12.5, Q3 18.5, maximum 33.0.
3. **Build the box** from Q1 (7.3) to Q3 (18.5), about 4 squares tall.
4. **Draw the median line** straight down through the box at 12.5.
5. **Draw the whiskers** — a line from the left side of the box out to 1.0, and from the right side out to 33.0. Cap each end with a short vertical tick.
6. **Label all five values** right on the plot, then title it: **Class Skimmer Launches, n = 147**.

{{< callout type="important" >}}
Two numbers land between grid lines. The median is 12.5, halfway between the 12 and 13 lines. Q1 is 7.3, just a hair right of the 7 line — about a third of one square. Neither gets rounded to the nearest square. This is the same move you just practiced on the ruler: the mark you want is often between two lines, and you place it where it actually falls. And the box does **not** get centered on the median — 7.3 to 12.5 is a shorter stretch than 12.5 to 18.5, and your box should show that.
{{< /callout >}}

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
The box holds the middle 50% of all 147 launches. If your box looks like it swallowed almost the whole number line, check that you plotted Q1 and Q3 — not the minimum and maximum.

## Closing

Talk about it at your table:

- The range is 32.0 ft, but the IQR is 11.3 ft. Both come from the same 147 launches. Which one better describes a typical launch, and what makes the other one misleading?
- The right whisker runs 18.5 to 33.0 — about 14.5 ft. The left whisker runs 1.0 to 7.3 — only 6.3 ft. What is happening in the class data that makes the right side so much longer?
- One student's three trials were 2.0, 24.0, and 25.0 ft. Their mean is 17.0 but their median is 24.0. Which number is the more honest report of what that skimmer can do, and why?

Then write in your notebook, on your own:

1. Use the box to finish this sentence: "About half of all class launches traveled between ____ and ____ feet, which means…"
2. Compare today's plot with the one you drew on Friday. Name one thing you got right and one thing you had to fix.
3. Write your own best launch distance. Does it land inside the box, out on a whisker, or past the end of one? What does that tell you about your skimmer compared to the rest of the class?
