Your checklist while the video plays:

## Work Session: Thumbnail Sketch and Measure

A thumbnail sketch is a fast, small, freehand drawing. No ruler, no straightedge, no erasing every line. Its job is to show the shape well enough to hang measurements on it. Engineers make dozens of these before they ever make a formal drawing.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
We are **not** doing full three-view drawings today. One quick sketch per object, with a few labeled dimensions. The formal TOP / FRONT / RIGHT work comes later this week.
{{< /callout >}}

For each object Mr. Willingham assigns:

1. **Name it** at the top of the sketch ("hex nut," "binder clip," "wood block").
2. **Sketch it freehand** — small, quick, roughly in proportion. If it is twice as wide as it is tall, draw it that way.
3. **Pick 2–3 dimensions worth measuring** — the ones someone would need to make the object. Usually a length, a width or diameter, and a thickness or depth.
4. **Measure each one with the caliper** and write it to three decimal places: 0.750 in., not "about 3/4."
5. **Label the dimension outside the sketch** with a short leader line pointing at the edge you measured. Nothing written on top of the object.
6. **State your units once** at the top of the page: *All measurements are in inches.*

Trade the tool. Switch who holds the caliper after every object — both partners measure, both partners sketch. Three good ones beats six rushed ones.

## Closing

Talk about it with your partner:

- Take one object you measured. What would the ruler have told you, and what did the caliper tell you? How big is the difference?
- Compare one of your readings to your partner's reading of the same edge. Identical? If not, what would cause two careful people to disagree?
- Name a job where being off by 0.01 in. would not matter at all, and one where it absolutely would.

Then write in your notebook, one or two sentences: **"I would use a dial caliper instead of a ruler when…"** Give a specific example from today, not a general statement.

Before you leave: caliper closed, wiped off, and back in its case. Objects back on the front table.
