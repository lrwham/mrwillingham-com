### Head the page

> **Activity 1.5 — Measuring Practice (Dial Caliper)**
> Date: 8/26/2026 · Partner: ________
> All measurements are in inches.

You are **not drawing anything** on this page. No sketch, no multiview, no cubes in the margin. This page is a list of labeled measurements and nothing else.

### With your partner

1. **Build it.** Use linking cubes to build the object shown in the photo and multiview sketch on the myPLTW page. It takes 20 cubes.
2. **Exterior width.** Take turns with the dial caliper. Measure the exterior width of the top piece and of the bottom piece.
3. **Interior width.** Now measure the interior width — the opening — of the top and bottom pieces. Use the **inside jaws**. This is the measurement a ruler cannot give you.
4. **External height.** Measure the external height of the top and bottom pieces.
5. **Check your numbers against another pair.** Where you disagree, measure again together before deciding who is right.

Write your six measurements like this — label every one, units on the page:

| Measurement | Your reading |
| --- | --- |
| Top piece — exterior width | ______ in. |
| Bottom piece — exterior width | ______ in. |
| Top piece — interior width | ______ in. |
| Bottom piece — interior width | ______ in. |
| Top piece — external height | ______ in. |
| Bottom piece — external height | ______ in. |

Four things that will cost you a reading:

- **Ignore the holes and the connector nubs.** Put the jaws on the flat faces.
- **Do not squeeze.** Close until the jaws just touch. Plastic gives, and a tight grip reads low.
- **Three decimal places.** Write 2.250 in. — not 2 1/4, not about 2.25.
- **A number with no label is not a measurement.** Every line says what it is.

## Work Session: Dimensioning

Start a second notebook page:

> **Activity 1.5 — Dimensioning a Multiview Sketch**
> Date: 8/26/2026
> Measurements are in inches.

Mr. Willingham hands out the multiview sketch (TOP, FRONT, RIGHT, ISOMETRIC) already drawn. Tape it into this page — **top edge only**, so your notebook still closes flat. You dimension the copy that is taped in.

### Steps

1. **Build it first.** Six linking cubes, assembled to match the sketch. You cannot dimension an object you have not held.
2. **Conventions.** We go through them together — the right way, then the six ways it goes wrong.
3. **FRONT view together.** Measure with the caliper, then place the dimensions with Mr. Willingham under the doc cam.
4. **TOP and RIGHT on your own.** Think before you write a number.
5. **Trade notebooks** with your partner and find **one** convention they broke. One, not a list.

### The six errors we grade for

1. **Duplicate** — the same measurement given in two views.
2. **Deducible** — a measurement the others already tell you. If the whole is 1.500 and one part is 0.750, the rest is known. Leave it off.
3. **On a hidden line** — a dimension pointing at a dashed edge. Move it to the view where that edge is solid.
4. **Inside the object** — every dimension line goes outside the outline.
5. **Not between adjacent views** — numbers stranded out at the edge of the paper. Put them in the space between two views.
6. **Crossing lines** — shortest dimension closest to the object, longer ones stacked outward, and leave a gap where an extension line starts.

### Key Vocabulary

Dimension Line
: The line with arrowheads that shows what length the number describes.

Extension Line
: The thin line that connects the object's edge to the dimension line, with a small gap where it leaves the object.

{{< callout type="important" >}}
**The hardest part of today: knowing when to stop.** A finished sketch of this object needs about five numbers. Most people's first attempt has fifteen. More dimensions is not better — it is wrong. Every extra number is one more place a mistake can hide, and when a drawing contradicts itself the shop stops.
{{< /callout >}}

Fair warning: on this object, the RIGHT view may need **no new dimensions at all.** If that feels wrong, check whether every number it would show is already somewhere else on the page.
