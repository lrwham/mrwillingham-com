## Work Session: What Makes a Front View the Best One?

Before you sketch anything, you decide which side of the object is the FRONT. That choice is not random — there are three tests, and the quiz expects you to know them:

1. **Most distinctive shape.** The front view shows the outline that makes the object recognizable — the profile you would draw if you could only draw one view.
2. **Longest feature.** The object's longest dimension runs across the front view, not into it.
3. **Natural resting position.** Draw the object the way it actually sits — a car on its wheels, not on its roof.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
Hidden lines do not disqualify a front view. A view with dashed lines can still be the best front view — the three tests decide, not the number of hidden lines. And "fewest edges to draw" is never a reason. If your front view fails the three tests, you picked the easy view, not the best one.
{{< /callout >}}

### Build it, then sketch it

Three rounds. Each round: build the object from linking cubes, pick the best front view using the three tests, then sketch TOP, FRONT, and RIGHT on graph paper — one grid square per cube face.

The alignment rules — not optional:

- TOP sits directly above FRONT — same width, lined up square for square.
- RIGHT sits directly beside FRONT — same height, lined up row for row.
- Solid lines for edges you can see; dashed lines for edges hidden inside the view.
- Label each view: TOP, FRONT, RIGHT.

**Round 1 — together.** We build the first object as a class and choose the front view out loud, test by test. Then everyone sketches all three views while Mr. Willingham draws under the doc cam. Match your paper to the screen.

**Round 2 — on your own.** New object. Build it, pick the front view, sketch all three views, then compare with your partner: did you pick the same front view? If not, whose passes more of the three tests?

**Round 3 — quiz format.** You get an isometric drawing plus the TOP and RIGHT views. The FRONT view is missing — you draw it. This is exactly how the quiz asks it. **No cubes this round:** read the two views you have, figure out what the front must look like, and draw it aligned.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
Stuck on Round 3? Work edge by edge: every vertical edge in the FRONT view must line up with an edge in the TOP view above it, and every horizontal edge must line up with one in the RIGHT view beside it. If a line in your front view has no partner in either neighbor, it should not be there.
{{< /callout >}}

## Closing: Trade and Check

Trade notebooks with your partner. Check their Round 2 sketch against this list and mark **exactly one** thing to fix:

- Do the three views line up, square for square?
- Does the front view pass the three tests?
- Are hidden edges dashed — and visible edges solid?
- Is every view labeled?

Then, on your own, one sentence at the bottom of the page: **"I knew ______ was the best front view of my Round 2 object because…"** Name at least one of the three tests in your answer.

Cubes fully apart and back in the bin. Tomorrow: build an object and draw all four views — isometric plus the three flat views — on the sketch sheet you will dimension next week.
