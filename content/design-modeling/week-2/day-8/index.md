## Work Session: The Three Primary Views

An isometric sketch shows you what an object **looks like**. A multiview sketch shows a builder what it **measures**. It does that with three flat, straight-on views — no angles, no 3D.

### Key Vocabulary

TOP View
: What you see looking straight down from above. Shows width and depth. Goes **above** the front view.

FRONT View
: What you see looking straight at the front. Shows width and height. This is the anchor — draw it first and build the other two around it.

RIGHT View
: What you see looking straight at the right side. Shows depth and height. Goes to the **right of** the front view.

### The alignment rule

This is the whole lesson. The three views are not three separate drawings scattered on a page. They line up.

| These two views | Share this | So they line up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FRONT and TOP | Width | Vertically — TOP sits directly above FRONT |
| FRONT and RIGHT | Height | Horizontally — RIGHT sits directly beside FRONT |

We watch the **Object Views** video together. There is no sound — that is not broken. Follow the shapes as the object rotates.

### Steps 7–10: Object 1

1. Sketch Object 1 **isometrically** on isometric graph paper, the same way as yesterday. Box it, then cut it. Count your cubes off the real model.
2. Cut out your isometric sketch and glue it in the **top-right corner** of a sheet of square graph paper.
3. Draw the **FRONT** view in the lower-left area of that sheet. Leave room above it and to the right — the other two views go there.
4. Draw the **TOP** view directly above the FRONT and the **RIGHT** view directly beside it. Use the graph paper squares to keep the alignment honest. **Label all three views.**

Check the Multiview Sketching resource on myPLTW any time you get stuck. Then add the finished sketch to your Engineering Notebook.

{{< callout type="important" >}}
Alignment is where almost everybody loses points. When you think you are done, **lay a ruler across your sketch.** Straight down from TOP to FRONT, the edges should meet. Straight across from FRONT to RIGHT, the edges should meet. If they do not, it is not finished.
{{< /callout >}}

### Career Connection: Grace Milton, sustainable packaging design

Grace Milton designs packaging. Every box, wrapper, and container she designs either creates waste or avoids it — so a package design decision is an environmental decision, at the scale of millions of units.

Her other point is the one worth writing down: engineers need both the technical knowledge **and** the professional skills to convey their ideas to other engineers and to business teams. A design nobody else can read does not get built.
