## Work Session: Draw, Check, Cut, Fold, Assemble

Fifty minutes, and the build has to be finished today so the glue can dry over the weekend. Pace yourself: roughly 17 minutes to draw all four parts and get in line for your check, then cut and fold, a few minutes to decorate while the parts are flat, then assemble. If you are past the halfway mark and still drawing, raise your hand — do not just keep working quietly.

### What to watch for

The instructions tell you what to do. These are the places where it goes wrong.

{{< callout type="important" >}}
**Dotted lines are FOLD lines, not cut lines.** You heard this yesterday. Cutting along a dotted line destroys the part.
{{< /callout >}}

- **Inches, in fractions.** Every dimension on the skimmer sketch is in inches. Line the folder up with the first hatch mark on your ruler, not the plastic edge.
- **Your two fins have to match each other.** Mismatched fins make a skimmer that turns instead of flying straight.
- **The air scoop is the fussy part.** Those 1/8 and 3/8 dimensions are how far in from the corners the fold lines sit. This is the piece that catches the air — small errors here show up on launch day.
- **Score before you fold.** Lay your ruler along the dotted line and run the ballpoint pen down it, pressing firmly without letting ink through. A scored fold is crisp; an unscored fold is a lumpy guess. Fold against the ruler, not freehand.
- **Cut on the line, not outside it.** An extra 1/16 of an inch on every edge adds up.
- **Thin line of glue, not a blob.** Too much tacky glue soaks the folder, warps the part, and takes all weekend to dry.

### Decorate your skimmer

About five minutes, and the timing is on purpose — your parts are cut and folded but not glued yet, so they lie flat and are easy to draw on. Once the glue is on, this gets much harder.

Markers and colored pencils are at the front. Name it, put a design on the fins, do a paint job down the body — it is your aircraft.

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
**Rules for decorating:** markers and colored pencils only. Nothing glued on, no stickers, no extra tape, nothing added to the nose or the fins. Every add-on changes the weight and the balance, and next week we compare flight distances as a class — the data only means something if the skimmers are built alike. Go easy on heavy marker near the nose; that is weight in the worst possible place.
{{< /callout >}}

### Assemble

Fins, then air scoop, then paper clip — in that order, so the tacky glue can set.

## Closing

Name on it. Lay it flat on the drying tray. Clean your station.

Your skimmer sits undisturbed all weekend — do not stack anything on it and do not carry it around. If you did not finish assembling, that is fine: leave your parts together in the tray with your name on them. You get time to finish next week before we launch.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
**Next week is launch week.** Three flights each, off the launcher, and we measure every one. Then we pool the whole class's distances and look at the range, the mean, and the median — and start asking why some skimmers went so much farther than others.
{{< /callout >}}
