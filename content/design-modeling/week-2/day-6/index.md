## Warmup: Describe It in Words

Pick up the object at your table — a stapler, a water bottle, whatever is there. Without drawing anything, write a description of it that would let someone who has never seen it build one. Trade with a neighbor. Could you build theirs from what they wrote?

That is why engineers draw.

## Work Session: The Four Types of Sketches

We watch the **Types of Sketches** video. In Entry #2, write the four types and one feature that identifies each:

### Key Vocabulary

Thumbnail Sketch
: Quick, small, freehand. Captures the shape and proportion of an idea in seconds.

Perspective Sketch
: Shows an object the way the eye sees it — lines converge toward a vanishing point. Good for showing a client what a product will look like.

Isometric Sketch
: A 3D sketch that shows three sides at once, with width and depth lines at 30 degrees and height lines vertical. Everything stays in true proportion.

Multiview Sketch
: Three flat, straight-on views (TOP, FRONT, RIGHT) lined up with each other. This is what a builder measures from.

Then discuss: which type shows a client what a toy will *look* like? Which shows a builder the exact parts? Different sketches do different jobs.

### Make a thumbnail sketch

Sketching is not art. The standard is **communication** — can someone else understand it? "I can't draw" is not a thing here.

Thumbnail sketches are quick, small, and proportional. They capture the details that matter and skip the ones that do not: block letters on a stapler, yes; wood grain on a pencil, no.

1. Pick a small, simple classroom object.
2. On square graph paper in your notebook, sketch it so the width and height are in proportion — if it is twice as wide as it is tall, draw it that way.
3. Add the basic details someone would need to recognize it.
4. Confident, dark lines. Do not fuss with it.

### Critique

Trade sketches with a partner. Answer both questions in your notebook, about *their* sketch:

1. Can you tell what the object is and how it is shaped?
2. What one thing would make it clearer?

Record the feedback you got under your own sketch.
