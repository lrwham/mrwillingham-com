## Warmup: Five-Number Summary

Head a new page in your Engineering Notebook: 9/28/2026 · **"Puzzle Cube Testing"**. There is no printout today. Everything in the warmup and the box plot review goes in your notebook.

Nine testers tried to solve a puzzle cube. Here are their times, in seconds:

**97 · 52 · 134 · 38 · 110 · 71 · 172 · 64 · 85**

### Finding the five numbers

1. **Put the times in order**, smallest to largest. Nothing else works until you do this. Count them: there should be 9.
2. **Minimum** — the first number in your ordered list. **Maximum** — the last number.
3. **Median (Q2)** — the middle number. With 9 numbers in order, that is the 5th one. It has 4 numbers below it and 4 above it.
4. **Q1** — the median of the numbers *below* the median. Do not include the median itself. That leaves 4 numbers. With an even count, add the two middle ones and divide by 2. Q1 does not have to be one of the numbers in the list.
5. **Q3** — the median of the numbers *above* the median. Leave the median out again, and use the same method as Q1.
6. **Write all five**, labeled, with the unit: seconds (s).

## Work Session: Box Plot Review

We draw this one together, by hand, in the notebook, right under your five-number summary.

1. **Draw the number line.** Use a ruler. It runs from **0 to 180** seconds, because 180 is the testing time limit. Put a tick mark every 10 seconds, evenly spaced, and label every 30: 0, 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180.
2. **Mark the five numbers.** Above the line, make a short vertical mark at the minimum, Q1, the median, Q3, and the maximum.
3. **Draw the box** from Q1 to Q3. The box is the middle half of the data.
4. **Draw a line through the box at the median.**
5. **Draw the whiskers** — a straight line from the left side of the box out to the minimum, and one from the right side out to the maximum.
6. **Label the number line** *Time (seconds)*.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
Read the plot back when you finish. A short box means the middle testers took about the same time. A long whisker means one or two testers were much faster or much slower than everyone else.

## Work Session: Team Testing

About 20 minutes are left. Your team starts testing its own cube.

{{< callout type="important" >}}
**Test your wooden cube** — the one your team glued before break. 3D-printed cubes go only to teams whose design was submitted correctly, and they will not be ready in time for testing.
{{< /callout >}}

Pick up your team's wooden cube and a **[Puzzle Cube Data Sheet](DM_PuzzleCube_TestingDataSheet.pdf)**. Everyone gets their own copy.

1. **Fill in the top of the sheet** — name, period, team number, class.
2. **Take the cube apart.** All the pieces go loose on the desk.
3. **Pick a tester and a timer.** The timer says "Go" and starts the clock. The clock stops when the 3×3×3 cube is closed.
4. **Three-minute limit.** If the cube is not solved at 180 seconds, stop. Write **180** and check the **DNF** box (Did Not Finish).
5. **Record Trial 1** — tester's name, team number, time in whole seconds — on every teammate's sheet.
6. **Take the cube apart again and run Trial 2** with a second teammate. On a team of two, switch jobs.

Testing rules — the same all week:

- Start with the cube taken apart, pieces on the desk.
- No hints from anyone.
- A tester who is waiting does not watch the cube get solved first.

**Done early?** Compare your warmup box plot with a partner's. If they don't match, find the step where they split.
