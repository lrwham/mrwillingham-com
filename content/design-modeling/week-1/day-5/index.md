## Warmup: You Were Already Engineers

Individual seats today. Yesterday you *were* engineers. Today you see the official process you followed without knowing it had a name.

We watch the **design process videos**. As each step comes up, fill in the matching row of your **Design Process Reflection Table**: what did your team do at that step? Be specific — "sketched three ideas in Entry #1" beats "brainstormed."

The six steps, in order:

1. Define the problem
2. Generate concepts
3. Develop a solution
4. Construct and test a prototype
5. Evaluate the solution
6. Present the solution

Then we talk: now that you can see the steps, what did your team do well? What would you do differently?

## Work Session: The Decision Matrix

Engineers rarely get to pick a design because they like it. They score it. A **decision matrix** lists the criteria down one side and the options across the top, gives each option a score for each criterion, and totals the rows. The highest total wins — even if it did not win every column.

We score the **Chair example** together first so you can see how it works. Then you score the class AFO prototypes on the **AFO Decision Matrix** and pick the one to send to the client, Ortho-Up.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
This is not a competition between teams. It is what real engineering teams do when they have more than one working solution and have to choose. The matrix makes the reasoning visible.
{{< /callout >}}

### Key Vocabulary

Decision Matrix
: A table that scores several solutions against the same criteria so the choice is based on evidence. Total each row; highest total wins.

## Closing: The Unit Problem

Two videos: the **Unit Problem** video (what cerebral palsy is, how it presents, how it is treated) and the **Problem Introduction** video. Here is what the whole quarter is building toward:

**Design a therapeutic toy for a child with cerebral palsy.**

Quick-write in your notebook: your first reaction, and one early toy idea. Do not overthink it — this is step 2, generate concepts, and bad ideas are allowed.

Next week: sketching. Thumbnail, isometric, multiview — the ways engineers draw an idea so someone else can build it.
