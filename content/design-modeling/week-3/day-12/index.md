## Work Session: Build It Square

### Three ways a skimmer goes wrong

Everyone in this class is building from the same dimensioned sketch. So when we plot how far everyone's skimmer flew, the whole spread comes from one thing: how accurately each person measured, cut, and folded. That starts today.

1. **Cutting a dotted line instead of folding it.** Dotted means fold. Once it is cut, the part is gone.
2. **Slits that are not a true 3 inches.** Too short or too long and the air scoop will not seat square on the body.
3. **Fins glued at different heights or angles.** This is the one that makes a skimmer curve off to the side instead of flying straight.

Two more from Friday that still apply: score before you fold, and a thin line of glue, not a blob.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
To check your fins, hold the skimmer up and sight straight down the body from the nose, like looking down a hallway. Both fins should look the same. Two seconds, and it catches the defect that matters most.
{{< /callout >}}

### Build

Pick up wherever you are: draw → check → cut → score and fold → assemble (fins, air scoop, paper clip) → decorate → name on it.

### Decorate and name it

Decorating happens after your build is square and finished, not instead of it. Markers and colored pencils only — nothing glued on, no stickers, no extra tape. Every add-on changes the weight and balance, and tomorrow we compare flight distances as a class.

**Your name goes on your skimmer.** An unnamed skimmer is a skimmer with no data behind it.

## Work Session: Launch Briefing

Tomorrow is a 30-minute period and it is pure testing. Everything gets explained today so that tomorrow you walk in and launch. Tools down for this part.

### The four non-negotiables

1. **Feet, always.** Nearest half foot, one decimal — 12.0, 15.5, 17.5. One unit for the whole class.
2. **Measure to the farthest end of the skimmer** — the very tip, wherever it ends up, even if it lands sideways.
3. **Three trials, no do-overs.** A bad launch is data. Record it.
4. **Write your own numbers.** The recorder calls it out; you write it in your notebook.

### The jobs in your lane

Everyone does every job — roles rotate each launch.

- **Lane Captain** — records every launch on the lane sheet.
- **Judges** (two) — agree on the distance to the farthest tip and call CLEAR before the next launch.
- **Alternate** — steps in wherever needed.

We launch one skimmer as a demo and the class measures it together.
