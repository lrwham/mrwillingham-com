## PLTW Gateway — Design and Modeling

Design and Modeling is a Project Lead The Way (PLTW) Gateway course offered in grade 7. Students discover the engineering design process and are challenged to apply it to design a therapeutic toy for a child with cerebral palsy. Along the way, students learn to communicate design ideas through sketching (thumbnail, perspective, isometric, and multiview), take accurate measurements in both customary and metric units, use statistics to make sense of test data, and use computer-aided design (CAD) software to build solid models of their designs. Students work in teams to research a problem, identify design requirements, build and test prototypes, and use data to refine their solutions.

The course meets for one quarter (nine weeks) and requires no prior experience.

### What we cover

- **Lesson 1: Introduction to Design** — the Foot Orthosis Instant Design Challenge, sketching (A Picture Is Worth a Thousand Words), measurement (Measuring Matters), Skimmer Statistics, and the dial caliper and dimensioning (Dialed In).
- **Lesson 2: Modeling** — solid modeling in Tinkercad (Taking Modeling to Another Dimension), surface area and volume (For Good Measure), It's for the Birds, and the Puzzle Cube Design Challenge.
- **Lesson 3: Design Challenge** — the Therapeutic Toy Design problem.

## Materials

Every student needs a composition notebook (wide-ruled, college-ruled, or quad) that becomes their **Engineering Notebook**, plus a pencil, pen, and colored pencils. Recycled build materials (shoe boxes, milk jugs, cereal boxes, foam, and similar) are collected as requested. Bring your student laptop and headphones every day. All other tools and technology are provided in class.

## Grading

Grades are in two categories: **summative (40%)** and **formative (60%)**. Formative grades are taken frequently and include learning checks, warmups, minor projects, and notebook entries. Summative grades are taken near the end of each unit of work and come in the form of projects and quizzes.

## Late Work Policy

Missing work will receive a grade of zero until it is submitted. Late work will be accepted with a single 20% deduction.

## The Standards

This course uses the Georgia Standards of Excellence for Middle School Engineering and Technology, course 21.02200 **Invention and Innovation — Grade 7** (MS-ENGR-II). Standards can be searched at [case.georgiastandards.org](https://case.georgiastandards.org/).

### MS-ENGR-II-1

Demonstrate employability skills required by business and industry.

- **MS-ENGR-II-1.1** — Communicate effectively through writing, speaking, listening, reading, and interpersonal abilities.
- **MS-ENGR-II-1.2** — Demonstrate creativity by asking challenging questions and applying innovative procedures and methods.
- **MS-ENGR-II-1.3** — Exhibit critical thinking and problem solving skills to locate, analyze and apply information in career planning and employment situations.
- **MS-ENGR-II-1.4** — Model work readiness traits required for success in the workplace including integrity, honesty, accountability, punctuality, time management, and respect for diversity.
- **MS-ENGR-II-1.5** — Apply the appropriate skill sets to be productive in a changing, technological, diverse workplace to be able to work independently and apply team work skills.
- **MS-ENGR-II-1.6** — Present a professional image through appearance, behavior and language.

### MS-ENGR-II-2

Demonstrate proper safety techniques and tool usage in the Engineering and Technology Laboratory.

- **MS-ENGR-II-2.1** — Accurately interpret and adhere to safety signs, symbols, and labels.
- **MS-ENGR-II-2.2** — Demonstrate and incorporate safe laboratory procedures in lab, shop, and field environments.
- **MS-ENGR-II-2.3** — Identify, select, and use appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), follow work area organization procedures and follow Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) when performing work.
- **MS-ENGR-II-2.4** — Identify, select, and use appropriate tools and machines for specific tasks.
- **MS-ENGR-II-2.5** — Demonstrate safe use of tools and machines.

### MS-ENGR-II-3

Investigate inventions and innovations and their impact in society.

- **MS-ENGR-II-3.1** — Students will differentiate between invention and innovation.
- **MS-ENGR-II-3.2** — Examine the role that Engineering & Technology and society play in the invention and innovation process.
- **MS-ENGR-II-3.3** — Examine the role that Engineering & Technology and society play in the invention and innovation process.
- **MS-ENGR-II-3.4** — Research an artifact related to Engineering and Technology that is at least 25 years old and how it has changed over time.
- **MS-ENGR-II-3.5** — Examine the patent process and the protection of intellectual property.

### MS-ENGR-II-4

Demonstrate an understanding of the Engineering Design Process through various problem-solving activities.

- **MS-ENGR-II-4.1** — Describe the steps of the Engineering Design Process.
- **MS-ENGR-II-4.2** — Construct a simple technological system.
- **MS-ENGR-II-4.3** — Explain how your technological system operates.
- **MS-ENGR-II-4.4** — Reverse engineer a consumer product.
- **MS-ENGR-II-4.5** — Utilize an Engineering Design Notebook as a record of process.

### MS-ENGR-II-5

Invent and/or innovate a technological product or system that addresses a societal need using the Engineering Design Process.

- **MS-ENGR-II-5.1** — Research a societal problem.
- **MS-ENGR-II-5.2** — Show evidence of the steps of the Engineering Design Process in an Engineering Design Notebook.
- **MS-ENGR-II-5.3** — Construct a prototype or model of the solution to the societal problem.
- **MS-ENGR-II-5.4** — Use mathematical and scientific reasoning as evidence to support an engineering solution.

### MS-ENGR-II-6

Explore how related career and technology student organizations are integral parts of career and technology education courses. Students will develop leadership, interpersonal, and problem-solving skills through participation in co-curricular activities associated with the Technology Student Association.
