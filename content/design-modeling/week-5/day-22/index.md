## Warmup: Five Numbers, By Hand

New notebook page, headed as always: name and period, 9/1/2026, title **Lesson 2.1 — Tinkercad**. Then, under the heading, this warmup. Five launch distances in centimeters:

**27 9 35 14 22**

Find Q1, Q2, and Q3, then the width of the middle 50%. Show your ordered list.

**Why five numbers matter:** Friday's warmup had seven values, so each half held three and the quartiles were just middle numbers. With five values, each half holds only **two** — so Q1 and Q3 are always the **average of two numbers**, and they can end in .5 and appear nowhere in your list. That is correct, not a mistake. Check your answers at the bottom of this page before you open a laptop.

### What Lesson 2 is about

For four weeks you have communicated designs the way engineers did for two centuries: pencil, grid paper, three views. Starting today you add the modern tool: **solid modeling** — building a true 3D object inside the computer that you can spin, view from any side, measure, and eventually print. The software is **Tinkercad**, a real CAD application used by real designers.

Nothing from Lesson 1 gets thrown away. A CAD model is built from multiview thinking — tomorrow you will literally build a Tinkercad object by reading a TOP/FRONT/RIGHT sketch, exactly the skill on Friday's quiz. And Lesson 2 ends with the **Puzzle Cube Design Challenge**, where your team designs, models, and builds a real puzzle.

### Warmup answer check

Ordered: 9, 14, 22, 27, 35. Q2 (median) = 22, the 3rd of five. Below Q2 sit two numbers, 9 and 14, so Q1 = (9 + 14) ÷ 2 = **11.5**. Above Q2 sit 27 and 35, so Q3 = (27 + 35) ÷ 2 = **31**. Middle 50% = 31 − 11.5 = **19.5 cm**.

Notice: 11.5 and 19.5 end in .5 and neither appears in the data. With five values, that is what correct looks like.
