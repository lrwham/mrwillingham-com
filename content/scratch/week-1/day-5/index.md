## Warmup: Maze Analysis

Today is Project Day 1 of the [Maze Game](/scratch/projects/maze-game/). Open it and look at the three example mazes, then answer in the CTLS discussion post:

1. Which maze is the easiest? The hardest? Why?
2. What makes a maze fun? What makes it frustrating?
3. How wide do the paths need to be for a small sprite to fit?

Read the **Design Rules** on the project page before the checkpoint.

{{% worksession %}}

## Work Session: Design, Swap, Build

Follow **Project Day 1** on the [Maze Game](/scratch/projects/maze-game/#project-day-1-maze-design) page:

1. **Design** your maze on the worksheet (about 8 minutes). Pick up a worksheet from Mr. Willingham.
2. **Swap** with your partner and review their maze (about 3 minutes). Write one piece of feedback on the back.
3. **Build** your partner's maze in Scratch: remix the starter project, fill the walls on the grid costume, and add a player sprite at the start.

{{% checkpoint %}}

## Standards

- [**MS-CS-FCP.4.1**](/scratch/description/#ms-cs-fcp4) — Develop a working vocabulary of programming including user interfaces and usability (paths wide enough for the sprite, clear start and finish).
- [**MS-CS-FCP.4.5**](/scratch/description/#ms-cs-fcp4) — Implement a simple algorithm in a computer program (recreating the paper design on the grid costume).
