## Warmup: What Is a Flow Diagram?

Yesterday you learned the vocabulary of conditionals. Today you **draw** that logic. Read the first two sections of [Flow Diagrams](/scratch/reference/flowcharts/): the three shapes (oval, rectangle, diamond) and how loops look.

Mr. Willingham walks through the "Is it raining?" example on the board. The diamond is the condition — the same thing that goes inside an `if` block.

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Warmup

- [ ] I know what the oval, rectangle, and diamond mean.
- [ ] I know that a diamond is a yes/no decision, like an `if` block.

{{% worksession %}}

## Work Session: Flow Diagram Worksheet

Pick up the [Flow Diagram Worksheet](/scratch/reference/flowcharts/worksheet/) and work with a partner.

### Part 1: Trace the Flow

Look at the maze wall-detection diagram on the screen. What is the condition? Where are the loops, and what repeats?

### Part 2: Draw Your Own

- **Diagram 1 — a real-life scenario.** A friend messages that they finished their homework and want to hang out. Draw at least three yes/no conditions, include a point where two paths converge, and (bonus) add a loop.
- **Diagram 2 — a game power-up.** Pick Speed Boost, Shield, or Invisibility (or invent one). Include a condition that activates it and a condition that ends it. See a student example: [Invisibility Power-Up](/scratch/reference/flowcharts/example/).

### Part 3: Trace Your Partner's Diagram

Swap both diagrams with another pair. Pick a starting input, trace every step, and write one piece of feedback. Did you reach an ending? Did anything break?

{{% checkpoint %}}

### Checkpoint: Work Session

- [ ] My Diagram 1 has at least three conditions and a converging path.
- [ ] My Diagram 2 has an activate condition and an end condition.
- [ ] I traced another pair's diagrams and gave feedback on each.
